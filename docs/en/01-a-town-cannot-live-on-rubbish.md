# A Town Cannot Live on Rubbish

*A call for Bosa to make something again*

---

Walk along the left bank of the Temo and you pass a row of red trachyte gables, shoulder to shoulder, facing the water. Sas Conzas. The tanneries.

People tanned hides here when Bosa was a Roman town. For almost a century, from the mid-1800s, this small place in the Planargia was the tanning capital of Italy, its leather sold across the peninsula and abroad. In 1924 a Bosa tannery took the Grand Prix and a gold medal at the international fair in Rome.

Then count. In 1834 there were twenty-eight tanneries. In 1860, twenty-three. In 1887, fifteen. In 1930, five. By 1950, one. In 1962, none.

In 1989 the buildings were declared a national monument.

**Bosa protected the buildings. It did not protect the work.**

## What the buildings do now

Today the conce are restaurants, bars, holiday homes, a museum, a supermarket. The ones that found no buyer are slowly falling in. The official story of why tanning ended is worth reading twice: the process was unhealthy, and the smell was no longer compatible with the town's *new tourist vocation*.

That is the moment Bosa stopped making things and started being looked at.

## Look a little deeper

Bosa has a new zona artigianale. The name promises artisans. Its two biggest occupants are Superpan and Eurospin supermarket chains. The rest are small service businesses. It is a zone for selling things made somewhere else.

Now watch what leaves the valley. Every day, lorries climb out of Bosa fully loaded, bound for Macomer. They carry rubbish. **Bosa's only export is its waste.**

Bosa was built as a port. The mouth of the Temo was the trading door of the whole Planargia; as late as the early 1800s the town was the depot for the produce of the surrounding hills, and the goods of the Tyrrhenian came in the other way. Bosa Marina is still designated as a 'commercial quay' but has not handled a cargo for more than 50 years. Today even the fishing boats have nearly all disappeared, replaced by yachts and day boats.

The narrow-gauge railway to Macomer opened in 1888–89 and brought Bosa a century of growth. Regular service ended on 14 June 1981, when the track was too worn to run. Since the mid-1990s it has been a summer tourist line only. It carries no goods and no products. Even the tourist train has stalled: fires in 2021 destroyed thousands of sleepers between Tresnuraghes and Bosa Marina.

So add it up. A port with no cargo. A railway with no freight. An industrial zone with no industry. A historic factory district with no factory. And one full lorry, every day, leaving Bosa with something that nobody wanted.

## What this means

A town in this condition is not an economy. It is a stage set. It lives on pensions, transfers and three summer months of tourists, and it sends its money out of the valley at the supermarket till and its rubbish out of the valley by truck. Everything else it consumes arrives from elsewhere.

A place that produces nothing has nothing to trade, and a place with nothing to trade has no say in its own future. It can only wait to be visited, funded, or emptied.

**A town cannot survive without producing anything.**

## What does Bosa's future hold?

This is not a plea for nostalgia. Nobody wants the old tanneries pouring chemicals into the Temo again. What Bosa needs is production that fits the town as it is now — clean, small, skilled, and locally owned.

1. **Put work back into the conce.** At least one tannery building returned to making things, as a working workshop, not a museum of work.
2. **Make the zona artigianale artigianale.** Reserve space and terms for people who make things, not only for people who sell them.
3. **Send something out of the valley that isn't rubbish.** Bosa once tanned hides from across Sardinia; today it has neither the hides nor the tannery. It has olive groves and no mill, a river and no trade. Olive oil, malvasia, food, crafts: something must leave full.
4. **Let the port and the railway carry goods again**, even small ones, even once a year.
5. **Keep a ledger of the valley.** Count what comes in, what goes out, and what is made here. What is not counted cannot be changed.

## Bosa, make something

The Romans tanned here. The Ligurian merchants traded here. Generations of Bosani stood in the vats up to their knees in water and lime. This generation sells coffee to visitors and exports its rubbish.

That is not a heritage. It is an ending — and it is one Bosa can still refuse.
