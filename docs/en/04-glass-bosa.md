# GLASS Bosa

*A worked example: applying the production model to the town's glass*

---

The first three texts set out why Bosa needs to produce again, how a production model should operate, and where production could happen. This fourth text takes one real material — the town's waste glass — and runs it through everything decided so far, step by step. It is a worked example, not a finished business plan. Where a number is an estimate, it says so.

## 1. The material: Bosa's glass

Bosa collects more glass than any other recyclable material except organic waste. According to ISPRA's national waste register, in 2024 the town collected **596 tonnes** of glass: about **81 kg for every resident**, or roughly 1.2–1.5 million bottles a year.

| Year | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glass (t) | 359 | 366 | 491 | 498 | 516 | 501 | 581 | 604 | 620 | 596 |

Glass is rising, unlike plastic. It is up about two thirds since 2015. It follows bars, restaurants and holiday lets more closely than households, so it is largely a measure of hospitality. All of it leaves the valley, and sending it away is a cost: in 2024 Bosa spent about €0.21 to collect and treat each kilo of separated waste, glass included (ISPRA). That is about €17 a year for each resident's glass, paid through TARI. Each kilo cast in Bosa is a kilo the town no longer pays to send away, and a raw material delivered for free.

## 2. Why glass, and not plastic

Plastic was the first candidate, and it failed the materials test. Household plastic is a mix of polymers that cannot be reliably told apart by eye. Mixed batches fail, and melting the wrong plastic at the wrong temperature releases harmful fumes. Making it safe would need reliable sorting, controlled heating and extraction — too much risk for a first product.

Glass is different. It is a single material, chemically inert, gives off no toxic fumes when heated, and its colour is visible. The real hazard is dust when crushing, which is managed with wet methods and masks.

## 3. The product: a recycled, verifiable commodity

The product is not an ornament made from waste. It is a **commodity**: Bosa's glass, fused with Bosa's sunshine and Bosa's work, weighed, marked and registered, and priced in euros by a market.

It promises nothing. It is not a voucher, a currency or a claim on anything held elsewhere. It is a certified piece of glass with known origin, weight, energy and work.

### Two units

| Unit | Name | Form | Glass | Colour | Relation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B** | Bosana | Cast half olive, flat back, hole at the stem end | 10 g | Green | 100 B = 1 BAR |
| **BAR** | Ingot | Cast bar in gold-bar proportions, about 22 × 7 × 3.3 cm | 1 kg | Brown | 1 BAR = 100 B |

The B is named after the Bosana, Sardinia's most common olive variety. A real Bosana weighs about 2.5–3.7 g; at 10 g of glass, the B is a little larger, about the size of a table olive. The weight is fixed, not the size.

**The BAR takes the shape of a gold bar.** It follows the proportions of the 400 oz bar that central banks hold, scaled to 1 kg of glass: a trapezoid with sloping sides, about 217 × 68 mm on top, 196 × 47 mm at the base and 33 mm high. The sloping sides release it from the mould, as they do for gold, and the 33 mm thickness keeps the anneal manageable.

**The B can be worn.** A fine hole, about 1.5–2 mm, runs across the olive at the stem end, so it hangs as an olive hangs from its twig. The hole is cast in: a refractory pin, of rolled ceramic fibre paper or thin ceramic rod, sits in each mould cavity and is removed after firing. Drilling with a diamond bit under water is the fallback. A silver setting, with no hole, is kept for a later jewellery edition.

### Markings

- **BAR:** sandblasted on the top face after casting, where a gold bar carries its stamps — *BAR · 1 kg · Made in Bosa*, the glass source, a unique serial number and the year. The base is left as it comes from the mould.
- **B:** too small to carry all the text. The mould gives a letter B mark on the flat back; the serial is sandblasted beside it. *Made in Bosa* and the weight go on the card that accompanies it. The hole at the stem end is kept clear of both marks.
- **Serial format:** for example `BAR-E0-001` and `B-E0-001` (unit, edition, number).
- **Tolerance:** each BAR is finished to 1,000 g ± 5 g; each B to 10 g ± 0.2 g, weighed after the hole is made. The actual weight of every piece is recorded.
- **Optional security feature, the copper rove:** a boatbuilder's copper rove, tapped flat, is laid on the floor of the mould before casting, roughly central and at least 10 mm from any edge. It ends up sealed into the base and shows through it as a copper outline with a hole in the middle. It drifts a little as the glass flows, so its final position is different on every bar. After casting, the centre of the hole is measured from a fixed corner of the base (the corner under the start of the serial), with a clear millimetre grid, to the nearest 0.5 mm, and entered in the register as X and Y. Anyone can check it by hand. It does not stop a deliberate copy, but with the serial and the weight it gives a third simple check.

The serial and the register entry are made at the same moment. That is what separates a B or a BAR from any other piece of green or brown glass: anyone can melt a bottle, but only registered pieces are *Made in Bosa*.

## 4. The decision framework, applied

Every product proposal goes through the same six tests. This is how glass performs.

| Test | Result | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Materials** | Passes | One material, from the valley, collected by source. Bottles from different makers can differ, so each source is tested and kept separate. |
| **2. Energy** | Passes | An electric kiln run on solar. About 2–3 kWh per kg in a small kiln (estimate, to be measured). |
| **3. Water and waste** | Passes | Inert material, no toxic fumes. Wash water and crushing dust are managed with simple methods. Nothing harmful left behind. |
| **4. Place** | Passes | A kiln fits a workshop. Crushing is noisy, so it belongs in the zona artigianale or a farm building, not the historic centre. |
| **5. Skills** | Passes, with training | Kiln casting is an established, learnable craft. It is held by more than one person from the start. |
| **6. The model** | Passes | Pre-ordered before it is made, sold all year, no public money needed. |

## 5. Does it fit the model?

| The model is | How glass fits |
| --- | --- |
| A cooperative enterprise that makes a profit | Each piece is sold above its production cost from the first edition. |
| A producer of goods | The product is a physical commodity, not a service. |
| Running its own services in-house | Collection, washing, crushing, the register and the market are run by the cooperative to serve production. |
| Never grant-dependent | Edition 0 is funded by its own auction. Later equipment is bought from income or members' capital. |
| Producing against confirmed demand | Nothing is cast without an order. |
| Growing person to person | Through the Glass Exchange and buyers who know the town. |
| Majority locally owned | Local members, local glass, local energy, local work. |
| Working all year | The material comes all year; casting runs all year. Collection peaks in summer, so material can be stockpiled for winter casting. |
| Built to get stronger from shocks | Many small sources, standard units, a modular kiln set-up, and skills shared from the start. |

## 6. The process, step by step

1. **Source.** The cooperative agrees with a bar, restaurant or winery to take a set of bottles of one type and colour. Green for B, brown for BAR.
2. **Handover.** The bottles are given to the cooperative **before** the business discards them, under a short written agreement. They are goods, not waste, and the Comune's collection service is not involved.
3. **Collect.** Very small-scale private collection by the cooperative.
4. **Wash.** Labels, caps and residue removed.
5. **Crush and sieve.** Into ready-to-fire glass (cullet) in two or three grades: chunks for ingots, fine glass for olives. Wet methods and masks against dust.
6. **Pack and label.** Bagged and weighed, labelled with source and date. The record starts here.
7. **Cast.** In a kiln, at about 820 °C, in moulds, followed by a slow anneal.
8. **Finish.** Edges ground, kiln wash removed, pieces cleaned.
9. **Weigh and mark.** Finished to tolerance, weighed, serial sandblasted.
10. **Register.** One entry per piece: serial, unit, actual weight, glass source, date cast, rove position if any, and date sold. Energy, glass loaded, minutes of work and rejects are recorded once per firing, in the firings log.
11. **Deliver.** To the person who pre-ordered it, with its card.
12. **Trade.** Holders can trade B and BAR with each other on the Glass Exchange.

## 7. What one piece embodies

The kiln's heat leaves the glass as it cools, so the piece does not store energy. It is **proof that energy and work were spent** on it.

Planning estimates for one BAR, from a solar-powered kiln:

|  | Per BAR | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | about 2–3 kWh | Estimate; replace with metered firings |
| Work | about 10 minutes | Estimate; replace with timed batches |
| Production cost | about €4 | Indicative; mostly labour |

Energy is a small share of the cost; most of it is human time. The selling floor of €10 per BAR (section 8) sits above this production cost, and the market decides the price above the floor.

## 8. Edition 0: the first batch

Edition 0 proves the process, measures the real numbers, and tests demand — with no equipment bought.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Pieces | **10 BAR** (brown) and **100 B** (green), each numbered |
| Finished glass | 11 kg |
| Crushed glass to prepare | about 15–16 kg, allowing for crushing loss, finishing, rejects and a test firing |
| Bottles | about 40, from one green source and one brown source |
| Production | an existing Sardinian glass studio with a fusing kiln |
| Funding | an auction of 20 numbered lots, held in a former tannery in Sas Conzas |

Two Sardinian glass workshops that work with kilns have been identified as first contacts, in Nuoro and Porto Torres.

### What the studio does, and what the cooperative supplies

- **The cooperative supplies:** washed, crushed and labelled glass by source; the design sheet (dimensions, weights, tolerances, markings); real Bosana olives for the master.
- **The studio provides:** a test firing for each glass source; a trapezoidal open-face ingot mould; a reusable multi-cavity mould for half olives, with a pin in each cavity for the hole; firing, annealing and finishing.
- **The moulds and the olive master** belong to the cooperative.

### What must be measured

Edition 0 is also the specification for everything after it. For every firing, the studio records:

- kWh used (from a meter);
- weight of glass loaded, and weight of finished pieces;
- the firing schedule;
- minutes of work per piece;
- rejects, and why.

### Two kinds of price

Nobody yet knows what B and BAR are worth. So the price is not guessed; it is discovered.

- **The cost floor** protects production permanently. Nothing is ever sold below what it costs to make: **€10 per BAR** and **€0.50 per B**. The B costs far more per gram than the BAR because each 10 g piece is cast, finished, serialled and certified by hand. So 100 B = 1 BAR by weight, not by price.
- **The market price** is whatever buyers pay above the floor. For a first edition it is set by auction; afterwards, on the Glass Exchange.

### The Edition 0 auction

Edition 0 is sold at auction in a gallery in one of the old tannery buildings of Sas Conzas — the place where Bosa last produced something. The same gallery is the first choice for the hub, so the auction is also the hub's opening night.

The gallery ran a successful auction in summer 2026: 20 works by anonymous artists, a €120 reserve, every work sold, many around €300 and the top close to €600. Edition 0 is close to what those buyers bought: numbered pieces with no famous name attached, from a story they know, in a room they know.

| Lots | Count | Reserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BAR-E0-002 to 010, one lot each | 9 | €120 each |
| B, in sets of 10 | 10 | €120 per set |
| **BAR-E0-001**, the last lot of the evening | 1 | €120 |
| **Total** | **20 lots** |  |

**What Edition 0 could raise**

| Scenario | Assumption | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | every lot at the €120 reserve | €2,400 |
| Typical | most lots around €250, BAR-E0-001 near €600 | about €5,300 |
| Strong | many lots near €300, BAR-E0-001 near €600 | about €6,300 |

In the typical case, Edition 0 pays for the cooperative's first kiln. Even at the floor, it pays for half of it. That is the model working as intended: the first product funds the first equipment, with no grants and no debt.

**On the night**

- The register is shown on a screen, and each piece's serial, glass source, kWh and minutes appear as it sells — a first look at the Glass Exchange.
- The process is shown in the room: bottles, crushed glass, moulds, photographs of the first firing.
- The hammer prices become the first public prices for B and BAR, and the opening prices on the Glass Exchange.

**Rules**

- Pieces are sold as **collectibles, not investments**. There is no promise that prices will rise.
- Nothing is auctioned that has not been made and registered.
- Twenty lots, no more. That is what the room has shown it can absorb.
- Later editions are separate and recorded. They do not dilute Edition 0, and later B and BAR can be sold nearer the floor so that the product stays within reach of the town.

## 9. The production unit, in stages

Production grows in stages. Each stage is paid for by the one before it, and a stage begins only when demand has outgrown the last.

| Stage | Kiln | Power | Capacity | Equipment cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Edition 0** | An existing studio's kiln | The studio's | 10 BAR + 100 B, once | none |
| **Stage 1** | 75-litre single-phase fusing kiln | EasySolar-II, about 4–5 kWp, battery | about 780 BAR a year, firing weekly | about €13,000–18,000 |
| **Stage 2** | Nabertherm GF 240, three-phase | 19.5 kWp, 15 kW inverter | about 6,500 BAR a year | only when demand needs it |

### Stage 1: the first unit

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Kiln chamber | 75 × 52 × 23 cm; floor 0.33 m²; 75 litres |
| Kiln supply | 3.6 kW, 230 V, single phase, 16 A |
| Maximum temperature | 950 °C (casting needs about 820 °C) |
| Size and weight | 95 × 88 × 68 cm; about 80 kg |
| Per firing | **15 BAR**, or about 12 BAR + 40 B |
| Per year | about 390 BAR firing fortnightly; **about 780 BAR** firing weekly |
| Energy per firing | about 35–55 kWh (estimate: small kilns use about 2–3 kWh per kg) |
| Energy per year | about 2–3 MWh, firing weekly |
| Inverter | Victron EasySolar-II 48/5000: inverter, solar charger and monitoring in one unit |
| Solar | about 4–5 kWp |
| Battery | about 10–15 kWh at 48 V |
| Backup | grid connection on the inverter's AC input |

**How it runs.** Each firing starts at dawn, so the high-power heating runs on direct sun. The long anneal and slow cool run overnight from the battery. The grid is there as backup, because an anneal must never stop halfway: a power cut during cooling cracks the ingots. The inverter's monitoring logs the kWh of every firing, which goes straight into the register. A supply contract above the usual 3 kW is needed, and the inverter runs close to its limit on hot summer days, so it needs shade and ventilation.

### Stage 1 equipment costs

| Item | Approximate cost |
| --- | --- |
| 75-litre kiln (offer found) | €4,800 incl. VAT |
| Victron EasySolar-II 48/5000 | €2,000–2,500 |
| Battery, 10–15 kWh at 48 V | €3,000–5,000 |
| Solar panels, about 4–5 kWp, with mounting | €2,000–3,000 |
| Electrical work, 16 A circuit, supply contract increase | €500–1,000 |
| Crusher, sieves, scale, moulds, sandblasting kit, masks | €1,000–2,000 |
| **Total** | **about €13,000–18,000** |

These are estimates to be replaced with quotes. A leaner start is possible: a smaller battery at first, with the grid covering more of the anneal, saves about €2,000–3,000.

**How it is paid for.** In the typical auction case, Edition 0 pays for the kiln. The rest comes from sales at or above the floor. Firing weekly, 12 BAR at €10 plus 40 B at €0.50–1 bring in about €140–160 per firing, or about €7,300–8,300 a year — but only for pieces actually ordered. The main running cost is work, at roughly €4 per BAR.

### Stage 2, and after

Stage 2 is a Nabertherm GF 240 (1010 × 810 × 400 mm, 0.82 m², 11 kW, three-phase) with 19.5 kWp of solar and a 15 kW three-phase inverter: about 40 BAR per firing and up to about 6,500 BAR a year, roughly 1% of Bosa's glass. It comes only when orders consistently exceed what Stage 1 can make.

After that, growth follows the officina diffusa principle: the next step is **another unit**, not a bigger kiln. In theory, about 90 Stage 2 units, with roughly 1.8 MWp of solar on 2–3 hectares, would handle the town's entire glass stream. That is a ceiling, not a target.

## 10. The Glass Exchange

B and BAR trade on the **Glass Exchange**: an exchange hosted in bars and cafés in Bosa, with a large screen showing the market — bids, offers, recent trades — where holders trade pieces directly with each other. The name is the material, the commodity and the thing you drink from. The Glass Exchange is not tied to one venue: any place in town that takes part can host it.

Every trade is a physical piece changing hands. The Glass Exchange never sells claims on pieces held elsewhere: no certificates, accounts or promises. That keeps it a commodity market.

## 11. The register and the dashboard

Every piece made is in a public register: serial, unit, finished weight, glass source, date cast, the rove's position (X, Y) for a BAR with a rove, and date sold. Each firing is logged separately, with its energy, glass loaded and rejects. Prices are not part of the register; they come from sales and the Glass Exchange. This is the transparent part. A public dashboard, GLASS Bosa, shows six things, in this order:

- **what glass costs Bosa:** the average cost to collect and treat one kilo of separated waste (about €0.21 in 2024, from ISPRA), what that means for each resident's glass (about €17 a year) and for the total waste bill (€263 per resident), and the saving from glass cast in Bosa instead;
- **the glass stream:** Bosa's total glass per year, and the same total per resident shown as a constant amount every month of the year (about 6.8 kg per resident per month in 2024);
- **one local production kiln:** the most one kiln can make, in BAR and B per firing and per year, and what share of the stream that would be at full capacity;
- **what is made and sold:** B and BAR made, B and BAR sold, glass cast in kilograms, and the share of the stream diverted so far;
- **prices:** the last, lowest and highest price of B and BAR against their floors, and every trade by date. The first prices are Edition 0's hammer prices; after that, the dashboard takes its prices from the Glass Exchange once it is live;
- **energy, in its own section:** the solar supply, metered kWh for every firing split into solar and grid, the share from solar, the firings that ran with no grid electricity at all (carbon free), and CO₂ avoided against firing the same kiln on the grid. A firing is called carbon free only when its grid meter reads zero.

These sit alongside the resilience measures of the production model: committed sales, off-season income, concentration, local ownership and shared skills.

## 12. Still to decide before Edition 0

1. **Who sells Edition 0**, and invoices it, until the cooperative exists.
2. **The auction terms** with the gallery: commission, payment, collection, and the date, set only once the pieces are made and registered.
3. **The handover agreement** with the first bars.
4. **Who checks and signs** each register entry, and a calibrated scale.
5. **The card or certificate** for each piece, and simple packaging.
6. **Studio terms:** cost, lead time, mould ownership, and the data to be recorded.
7. **Filing the *Made in Bosa* trademark**, before the first pieces go public.
8. **A photographic record** of the process, from bottle to piece.

## 13. Points to confirm

- **Waste status:** confirm in writing that bottles handed over before disposal are goods, not waste, and that regular collection from several businesses does not require waste-carrier registration.
- **Energy and work per piece:** replace the estimates with Edition 0's measured figures.
- **The olive hole:** check at the test firing that the pin stays in place while the glass settles; if it moves, drill instead.
- **The rove:** cast two or three flattened roves at the test firing; confirm the glass does not crack around them and that the hole can be read through the base.
- **The trading rules** of the Glass Exchange, so that it stays a market for physical goods.

---

Glass is the first worked example. The same steps — material, tests, model, process, measurement — apply to whatever Bosa decides to make next.
