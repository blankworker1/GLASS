# Made in Bosa

*Un'officina diffusa*

---

The first text asked what Bosa makes. The second set out how a production model should operate: a cooperative, making only what is already wanted, measured by resilience rather than size.

This third text asks where. Not where to build, but where things could be made now, using spaces, equipment and skills that Bosa already has and does not use.

## The idea: from albergo diffuso to officina diffusa

The albergo diffuso turned empty houses in historic centres into a single hotel. No new building, one reception desk, rooms spread across the town.

An officina diffusa does the same for production. Small workshops in existing buildings — a former conca, a garage, a cantina, a farm building, an idle unit in the zona artigianale — are joined into one coherent enterprise. Each workshop does its part. Together they make the product.

## Why it suits Bosa

- **It uses what is already there.** Bosa's largest surplus is empty and underused space. This model is built on it.
- **No single point of failure.** If one workshop stops, the others continue. No building, machine or person is indispensable.
- **It grows with demand.** Workshops come into use as orders arrive, without the fixed costs of a single large factory.
- **Anyone can contribute.** An empty garage, an unused machine or a skill can become part of the enterprise.
- **It brings production back into the town.** Not hidden away, but visible, in the places people walk past every day.

## How it works

### The hub

The hub is the equivalent of the albergo diffuso's reception desk. It is a product showroom where:

- products are shown and orders are taken;
- visitors and tourists come to see, taste and buy;
- the cooperative has its office and registered address.

There are three possible places for it:

1. **The existing art gallery in a former tannery in Sas Conzas.** Ready now, and the natural place to start.
2. **Part of one of the workshops, such as a brewery building.** Production and showroom under one roof, so visitors see things being made.
3. **One of the derelict buildings in Sas Conzas.** A building brought back into productive use, in the same place where Bosa once made leather.

The hub could start in one place and move later as the enterprise grows.

### The workshops

Each workshop is its own site, with its own permits, registered with the Comune. They share one standard of quality, one order book and one name: *Made in Bosa*.

### Made in Bosa: the collective trademark

*Made in Bosa* is registered by the cooperative as a collective trademark (*marchio collettivo*). It is not a logo anyone can use. It comes with written rules of use (*regolamento d'uso*) that set out:

- **who can use it:** member workshops that meet the standard;
- **what the standard is:** where and how things are made, which materials, what quality;
- **how it is checked:** and how the right to use it can be withdrawn.

The mark tells a customer three things: this was made in Bosa, by a member of the cooperative, to an agreed standard. It protects the workshops from imitation, and the customer from anything that only looks local.

It follows the idea of the Made in Italy label, applied to a single comune.

### The website

One website serves both sides of the enterprise.

- **Front of house, for customers:** the products, how to order, delivery schedules, and where each product is made.
- **Back of house, for the workshops:** incoming orders, production runs that pass through several sites, materials, and who does what, when.

### The map of production places

A map of every place in Bosa where something is made. For the workshops, it shows the network. For visitors, it becomes a route: *come and see where things are made in Bosa*. Tourism becomes the way people find the product, not the product itself.

## Making Bosa productive again

Bosa already has much of what it needs to begin: empty buildings, idle machines, materials that are thrown away, the sun, and people who still know how to make things. What it does not have yet is all of this written down in one place, and people ready to put it to work together.

If you are from Bosa and want the town to make something again, this is where to start. Participate in the census — add a space, a machine, a material, a skill, yours or someone else's. Say what you would make, or what you would buy if it were made here. And come to the next meeting to discuss your ideas with others.

The tanneries were built one workshop at a time. So will this be.
